In April I completed my Master’s Degree in Mathematics at the **University of Bonn** with distinction, where my work focused on the intersection of stochastic analysis and constructive quantum field theory. Currently, I am working along two parallel tracks: compiling a recent uniqueness result into a solo preprint, and shifting my focus from the mathematical foundations toward **computational mathematics**. 

My most recent research focused on the stochastic quantization of the $$\Phi^4_2$$-model. Specifically, on the interpolation between the underdamped and the overdamped Langevin quantization. This work mathematically proves that inertia can serve as a tunable parameter to balance state-space exploration: it maps how the underdamped regime yields fast mixing and rapid sample decorrelation at the cost of numerical stability, while the overdamped regime offers the inverse. During this work I also derived a theoretical proof, showing that the non-interacting scalar Euclidean Quantum Field Theory measure is the unique equilibrium state of the underdamped Langevin quantization of the mean-field limit of the $$\Phi^4_2$$-model.
